## Ranked objections

1. **Blocking. Semantic-unit reach is not comparable across substrates and is
   easy to game.**
   Quoted sentence: “Semantic-unit reach was 21.2%, 100%, and 12%.”
   Minimal fix: define frozen native unit types, split Minimal procedures from
   receipts, and state that the ratios are within-substrate coverage rather
   than comparable amounts of useful information.

2. **Blocking. The five expression axes drift across prose, table, and
   caption.**
   Quoted sentence: “The expression profile measures storage, semantic reach,
   adaptivity, runtime activation, and actor-callable capability.”
   Minimal fix: standardize on storage reach, eligible-payload reach,
   semantic-unit reach, selection adaptivity, and execution reach.

3. **Should-fix. “Actor-callable capability = 0” is ambiguous.**
   Quoted sentence: “All three lacked actor-callable retained capabilities.”
   Minimal fix: define an actor-visible invocation handle in the frozen action

   Quoted sentence: “Hybrid was the only adaptive substrate.”
   Minimal fix: call task conditioning an implementation property, call the
   gap coverage expansion or turnover, and disclaim relevance and benefit.

5. **Should-fix. Universal profile generalization is too broad.**
   Quoted sentence: “An expression profile can be reported for any persistent
   agent.”
   Minimal fix: scope this to systems with frozen inventories, eligibility
   rules, adapter emissions, and actor-visible actions; permit declared

## Cannot be fixed by language alone

- Cross-substrate information equivalence requires an external callable
  inventory or independent re-unitization study.
- The expression hypothesis requires a factorial native-interface
  intervention.
- Retrieval adaptivity requires a counterfactual task-permutation or randomized
  selection experiment.
- Strong sham inference requires preregistered token-, structure-, or
  semantic-matched shams with replication.
- Independent evolution claims require more than one development lineage.
